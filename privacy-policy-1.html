V Vault
Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 2026

V Vault does not upload, collect, or share your photos or videos. No server ever receives your photos, videos or backups, and there are no analytics. Your vault stays on your device. The only account involved is your own Google account, used to verify your email and, if you choose, to store encrypted backups in your own Google Drive. Everything below explains exactly what is stored and why.

What's stored, and where

Your photos, videos, folders, captions, and your password are saved only in this browser's local storage (IndexedDB), tied to this app on this device. Nothing leaves your device unless you connect Google Drive backup, and no V Vault server receives your photos, videos or backups.

Sign-in and your Google account

You can sign in with Google, or verify your Gmail address with a one-time code sent by email. When you use "Continue with Google", the app reads your Google account's email address and name to verify you, and asks permission to store backup files in your Google Drive. The email address is kept on your device with your vault settings.

V Vault only requests the Google Drive permission that limits access to files the app itself creates (the backup files). It cannot see the rest of your Drive. You can disconnect at any time from the Backup screen, or remove V Vault's access in your Google Account settings.

If you choose the email-code option, your email address is sent to an email delivery service (EmailJS) once, only to deliver the code.

One active phone per account

When you sign in with Google, V Vault can check which phone is currently signed in to that Google account, so that a second phone has to be approved by the first (like a "new device" prompt). For this a small account server (hosted on Cloudflare) stores, per Google account: your email address, a random ID of the active phone, a one-way hash of that phone's session secret, the time it was last active, and short-lived sign-in requests (the name of the requesting device, e.g. "Android phone").

Your Google access token is sent to that server once per sign-in only to ask Google whose account it is, and is never stored or logged. The server never receives your photos, videos, vault password or backups. Signing out frees the account immediately, and an account that has been inactive for a long time is freed automatically.

Your password

Your password is never stored as plain text. It's hashed using PBKDF2 (SHA-256, 150,000 iterations) with a random per-vault salt — only that hash and salt are saved. A forgotten password can be reset via your security question, entirely offline.

Backups

A backup file bundles your photos, albums, and captions as JSON, then encrypts the whole thing with AES-256-GCM using a key derived from your vault password. Your password itself is never written into the backup file.

Backups are saved to your own Google Drive account (the one you signed in with), in a folder created by the app, or to your device. Backups are encrypted before they leave your device, so Google only stores an unreadable file. Older backups beyond the most recent few are removed from that folder automatically. The developer never receives your backups.

Privacy blur

Vault content blurs instantly when the app loses focus, so nothing sensitive is visible in the app switcher or to someone glancing over your shoulder. This is a browser-level deterrent, not an OS-level screenshot block — a determined user could still capture the unlocked screen.

Limits to be aware of

  • Photos are not encrypted at rest in local storage — only backup files are encrypted.
  • Someone with direct access to your device's browser profile could potentially recover unencrypted content.
  • Clearing your browser's site data for this app permanently deletes your vault, unless you've made a backup first.

Changes to this policy

Since this app has no server and no update-delivery mechanism beyond the app itself, any change to how data is handled would ship as a new version of the app, with this page updated to match.

V Vault is offline-first. This page describes the app's current behavior and is not a substitute for reviewing the source code yourself.
